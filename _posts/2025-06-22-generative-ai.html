Generative AI is a family of conditional distribution models, not a single product category. Language models generate token sequences autoregressively; diffusion models learn to reverse a noising process; multimodal systems connect representations across text, image, audio, and action. Applications become reliable only when inference is surrounded by retrieval, validation, policy, and observability.

Autoregressive language generation

A causal language model factors a sequence into conditional next-token probabilities:

p(x_1,...,x_T) = product(t=1..T) p(x_t | x_<t)

Decoding chooses tokens from this distribution. Temperature changes concentration, top-k truncates to the k most likely tokens, and nucleus sampling keeps the smallest set whose cumulative probability exceeds p. Greedy decoding is deterministic but can repeat; sampling increases diversity but also risk. Structured output requires constrained decoding or post-generation validation, not a prompt alone.

Speculative decoding uses a smaller draft model to propose tokens and a larger model to verify them. It can reduce latency when the draft has high acceptance without changing the target distribution. KV caching, continuous batching, prefix caching, and quantization determine whether the theoretical model speed translates into useful throughput.

Diffusion and denoising

Diffusion models define a forward process that gradually adds noise and train a network to predict noise, velocity, or a clean sample. A simplified denoising objective is:

L = E_t,epsilon || epsilon - epsilon_theta(x_t, t, c) ||^2

At generation time, a sampler follows a sequence of reverse steps from noise toward a sample conditioned on text or another modality. Fewer sampling steps reduce latency but can change detail and composition. Classifier-free guidance increases condition adherence by combining conditional and unconditional predictions, often at the cost of artifacts when guidance is too high.

Controllability

Control signals can enter through cross-attention, adapters, masks, reference images, structural maps, or low-rank fine-tuning. Control mechanisms should be evaluated for prompt adherence, identity preservation, composition, safety, and robustness to ambiguous instructions. A visually attractive sample can still violate the user's constraint.

Evaluation

Generative evaluation is multi-dimensional. Measure factuality and citation support for language, prompt adherence and human preference for images, schema validity for structured outputs, and latency, cost, and refusal behavior for every modality. Include adversarial prompts, unanswerable questions, copyrighted or private data probes, and distribution-shift cases.

Automated judges can scale review but inherit model bias and may reward verbosity. Calibrate them against blinded human ratings and keep a curated regression set. Log model, adapter, prompt, sampler, seed where relevant, and policy versions without retaining unnecessary sensitive content.

Production architecture

A reliable generative application usually contains a model gateway, retrieval or tool layer, schema validator, safety policy, rate and token budgets, cache, and trace pipeline. Treat model output as untrusted data until it passes parsing and policy checks. Tool calls need typed schemas, authorization, idempotency, and human approval for irreversible actions.

Use fallbacks deliberately: a smaller model for low-risk requests, retrieval-only responses when evidence is missing, or an abstention when confidence and grounding are insufficient. Generative AI is not production-ready because it can create convincing output; it is ready when the system can bound, test, explain, and recover from that output.
