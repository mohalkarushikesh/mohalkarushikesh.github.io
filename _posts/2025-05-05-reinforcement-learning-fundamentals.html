Reinforcement learning optimizes behavior through delayed consequences. The difficulty is not merely choosing an algorithm: the agent must learn from correlated trajectories, estimate uncertain returns, explore without catastrophic actions, and operate under a policy that remains safe outside the training environment.

The control problem

A Markov decision process contains states, actions, transitions, rewards, and a discount factor. The objective is the expected discounted return:

J(pi) = E_pi[ sum(t=0..T) gamma^t r_t ]

In partially observed environments the policy receives observations rather than the true state, so recurrent memory or a learned belief representation may be needed. Reward design is a specification problem: an omitted constraint is often interpreted as permission to exploit it.

Policy gradients

Policy-gradient methods optimize a parameterized distribution directly. The score-function estimator is:

grad J(theta) = E[ grad log pi_theta(a|s) * A(s,a) ]

The advantage compares an action with the value expected from the state. A learned critic reduces variance, but critic error can bias the actor update. Normalize advantages per batch only when the resulting change is understood and evaluated.

PPO and advantage estimation

Proximal Policy Optimization limits how far a new policy moves from the behavior policy:

r_t(theta) = pi_theta(a_t|s_t) / pi_old(a_t|s_t)
L_clip = min(r_t A_t, clip(r_t, 1-epsilon, 1+epsilon) A_t)

Generalized Advantage Estimation uses a lambda-weighted sum of temporal-difference residuals to trade bias for variance. PPO is not automatically stable: rollout freshness, value clipping, entropy pressure, reward scale, episode truncation, and environment determinism all affect results.

Offline and preference-based RL

Offline RL learns from a fixed dataset without new environment interaction. The central hazard is extrapolation error: a critic assigns high value to actions that are poorly represented in the data. Conservative objectives penalize unsupported actions or constrain the learned policy toward the behavior distribution.

Preference optimization uses comparisons instead of absolute rewards. Direct Preference Optimization increases the likelihood of a preferred response relative to a rejected response under a reference policy, reducing the need to train a separate reward model. It still depends on preference quality, coverage, and resistance to reward hacking.

Safe deployment

Evaluate constraint violations, worst-case episodes, recovery behavior, observation perturbations, and performance under altered dynamics. Use action shields, safety critics, constrained optimization, or a human approval layer when an unsafe action has irreversible cost.

Before deployment, run the policy in simulation with randomized environments, then shadow it against a trusted controller. Log observations, actions, policy version, value estimates, and intervention reasons. Monitor behavior drift and keep a tested rollback policy. High average return is not enough if the tail contains catastrophic behavior.
